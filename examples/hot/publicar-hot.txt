DESCRIPTION:
The air around a lightning bolt can reach about 50,000°F, about five times hotter than the surface of the sun. Here are six of the hottest things, ranked: Death Valley's record air, Mercury's sunny side, Venus, the sun's surface, lightning, and the sun's core.

Notes:
- Death Valley's 134°F (July 10, 1913) is the highest air temperature on record, certified by the WMO in 2012. Some researchers question the 1913 reading.
- Mercury's sunny side reaches about 800°F (~430°C). Venus averages about 860°F (~465°C) day and night, hotter than Mercury because its thick carbon dioxide air traps heat.
- The sun's surface is about 10,000°F; its core is more than 27 million°F (NASA).
- The ~50,000°F figure for lightning is the air heated around the bolt, and only for a moment.
